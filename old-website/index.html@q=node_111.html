the election 2007 in Poland

Submitted by Piotr Kodzis on Fri, 2007-11-30 15:20.
  • who's using akk@da

Upon orders from the Polish National Electoral Commission, a company called MIKROBIT has designed, implemented and maintained the official national web page containing the results of the parliamentary elections of The Republic of Poland ordered for the day of 21st October 2007. This internet web site (http://www.wybory2007.pkw.gov.pl/) provides full information about the elections in 2007 in Poland. This includes the lists of election committees and candidates, electoral bodies, turnout, general and detailed results and a lot of statistical data.

AKK@DA was chosen by MIKROBIT specialists to quickly detect faults and monitor the performance of the complicated system of the visualization of the national elections results on the internet. The main goal of using AKK@DA was to keep an eye on every single piece of the system and to be sure everything worked well. Monitoring covered network, servers, functioning of the specialized software written by MIKROBIT and data exchange between MIKROBIT and the National Electoral Commission. Except for fault monitoring, MIKROBIT’s specialists expected to have a good tool for performance monitoring, which would allow them to proactively control their system and react before any failure occured, as well as keep performance statistics to share with telecommunication providers and the National Electoral Commission in the future. AKK@DA as an open source software was also attractive to them because it didn’t raise the costs of this project.

The 2007 elections in Poland were called suddenly. The parliament chose to terminate itself two years before the constitutional term required. The suddeness of that situation allowed MIKROBIT only a few weeks weeks to prepare the startup of the visualisation system. That was the next reason why AKK@DA was chosen because AKK@DA's installation and implementation, including all necessary customization for a specific monitored environment, are possible to be done very quickly. AKK@DA doesn’t need a long and laborious process of monitoring the system configuration. It detects and automatically starts to monitor all available supported services and resources.

As was mentioned before, the visualization system of the elections results needed specialized, proprietary MIKROBIT software, which based on data provided by the National Electoral Commission had to generate and maintain updated information about results on the Internet. Through the flexible and easily extendable design of AKK@DA, it was possible to add support for specific MIKROBIT software very quickly. This allowed MIKROBIT’s specialists to have a full perspective of the status of every aspect of their system and be sure that everything worked well.
